FORGING CALCULATORS / TAPER GEOMETRY

Taper Forging Calculator

Calculate the stock required for square, rectangular, and wedge-shaped tapers using volume-conserving geometry.

How the Calculation Works

A forged taper changes cross-sectional area along its length. As steel is drawn out, the material is redistributed. Assuming no loss, the volume of the original stock equals the volume of the finished taper.

1. Calculate the starting cross-sectional area

For square or rectangular stock, multiply width by thickness:

Astarting = W × H

W = starting width; H = starting thickness; A = cross-sectional area.

2. Calculate the volume of the finished taper

If width and thickness each change uniformly (linearly) from the starting dimensions to the tip dimensions, the average cross-sectional area is:

Aaverage = (2WH + Wth + Htw + 2twth) ÷ 6

tw = tip width; th = tip thickness. Multiply average area by finished taper length to obtain volume:

Vtaper = Aaverage × Ltaper

Unlike a uniform bar, a taper does not have the same area everywhere along its length.

3. Calculate the original stock required

Divide the finished taper volume by the starting cross-sectional area:

Lstock = Vtaper ÷ (W × H)

Example: Forge 1" × 1" square stock into a 6" long taper ending at a ¼" × ¼" tip.

Starting area: 1 × 1 = 1 in²

Average taper area: (2 + 0.25 + 0.25 + 0.125) ÷ 6 = 0.4375 in²

Finished volume: 0.4375 × 6 = 2.625 in³

Original stock required: 2.625 ÷ 1 = 2.625"

Additional reference: point tapers, wedges, and material allowance

4. Common taper relationships

For a four-sided square taper drawn to a point, both width and thickness reach zero at the tip:

Lstock = Ltaper ÷ 3

For a wedge taper, where one dimension remains constant and the other decreases linearly to zero:

Lstock = Ltaper ÷ 2

These simplified relationships apply only to the specified tip geometry.

5. Account for material allowance

Additional stock can be prepared for scale loss and cleanup:

Lprepare = Lstock × (1 + P ÷ 100)

P = extra allowance percentage. For the example above, with 5% allowance:

2.625" × 1.05 = 2.756" to prepare.

Important: The model assumes a straight taper with width and thickness changing linearly, uniform starting stock, and conservation of volume. Real forging can introduce uneven deformation, scale loss, and dimensional variation.
