Upsetting Calculator

Estimate cross-sectional swell when a length of steel is compressed during upsetting. Compare uniform upsetting with an idealized localized bulge. All dimensions are in inches.

How the Calculation Works

Upsetting shortens a heated section of bar while increasing its cross-sectional area. Assuming no material loss, the volume before and after forging is equal.

A₀ × L₀ = Af × Lf

A₀ = original cross-sectional area; L₀ = original heated length; Af = final average area; Lf = final upset length.

Uniform upsetting

For round stock, diameter grows with the square root of the length ratio. For square stock, the same relationship applies to side width if the cross-section remains square.

Df = D₀ √(L₀ / Lf)
Wf = W₀ √(L₀ / Lf)
Example: A 1" round bar upset from a 4" section to 3" has an average final diameter of √(4/3) = 1.155", assuming uniform deformation.
Additional reference: localized bulging and assumptions

In localized mode, the visualization uses a broad-shouldered barrel profile. The modeled cross-sectional area increases through smooth shoulder transitions and stays relatively full across the middle of the upset section. The profile is normalized so the integrated volume equals the original stock volume. The peak width is model-dependent and is not a prediction of the actual forged shape.

For round stock, the displayed width represents diameter. For square stock, it represents an equivalent square width rather than the actual shape of the corners.

Real deformation depends on temperature distribution, friction, hammer or die contact, stock orientation, and restraint. Severe upsetting can buckle or fold rather than produce the smooth form shown here.
