FORGING CALCULATORS / TENON GEOMETRY

Tenon Calculator

Calculate the original bar length required to forge square or round tenons using volume-conserving geometry. All dimensions are in inches.

How the Calculation Works

A tenon is a reduced section forged at the end of a bar. Because the smaller tenon has less cross-sectional area, a shorter portion of the original bar can be drawn into the required finished length. The calculation uses conservation of volume.

1. Calculate the starting stock area

The starting cross-sectional area depends on the stock shape:

Square: A = W²
Rectangle: A = W × H
Round: A = πD² ÷ 4

W = width; H = thickness; D = diameter.

2. Calculate the finished tenon volume

Multiply the tenon's cross-sectional area by its finished length:

Vtenon = Atenon × Ltenon

For a round tenon, Atenon = πD² ÷ 4. For a square tenon, Atenon = W².

3. Calculate the original stock length required

Divide the tenon volume by the starting stock area:

Lstock = Vtenon ÷ Astock

Example: Forge a ½" diameter round tenon, 2½" long, from 1" square stock.

Starting area: 1 × 1 = 1.000 in²

Tenon area: π × 0.5² ÷ 4 = 0.19635 in²

Tenon volume: 0.19635 × 2.5 = 0.49087 in³

Original stock length: 0.49087 ÷ 1 = 0.491"

Additional reference: square tenons and material allowance

4. Square tenon example

A ½" square tenon, 2½" long, forged from 1" square stock:

Lstock = (0.5² × 2.5) ÷ 1² = 0.625"

5. Add extra material allowance

Lprepare = Lstock × (1 + P ÷ 100)

P = extra allowance percentage. For the round-tenon example with 5% allowance: 0.49087 × 1.05 = 0.515" to allocate.

Important: The result is the theoretical original length of material allocated to the tenon, not the length of the entire workpiece. It does not model shoulder transitions, localized upsetting, scale loss, or deformation.
