How the Calculation Works

When forging steel into a swage or shaped die, the material is redistributed to match the finished cross-section. Assuming the forged section maintains the same length, the starting stock and finished profile must have equal cross-sectional areas. This calculation is based on the principle of conservation of volume.

1. Calculate the swage cross-sectional area

The cross-sectional area depends on the selected swage geometry. For a half-round profile:

A = πW² ÷ 8

A = cross-sectional area; W = swage width (diameter of the semicircle).

2. Calculate equivalent starting stock

Once the swage area is known, the equivalent dimensions for square and round starting stock can be calculated.

Square: S = √A
Round: D = √(4A ÷ π)

S = square stock side; D = round stock diameter. The calculator compares these theoretical dimensions with common fractional stock sizes.

3. Compare starting stock and swage area

The difference between the starting stock area and finished swage area indicates whether material is theoretically excessive or insufficient at an unchanged forged length.

ΔA = Astock − Aswage

A positive result indicates excess material that may form flash. A negative result indicates insufficient material to fill the finished cross-section under the stated assumptions.

Example: A 1" wide half-round swage formed from 5/8" square stock.

Swage area: π × 1² ÷ 8 = 0.3927 in²

Starting stock area: 0.625² = 0.3906 in²

Area difference: 0.3906 − 0.3927 = −0.0021 in²

The starting stock contains approximately 99.5% of the area required for the finished half-round profile.

Additional reference: swage geometries and material balance

Other Swage Profiles

V / Triangle: A = W × D ÷ 2
Trapezoid: A = (W + B) × D ÷ 2
Rounded-Bottom U: A = WH + πW² ÷ 8

W = profile width; D = depth; B = bottom width; H = straight-wall height.

Estimated Flash

Flash Area = max(0, Astock − Aswage)
Flash Width Per Side = Flash Area ÷ (2 × Flash Thickness)

Unfilled Cavity

Unfilled % = [max(0, Aswage − Astock) ÷ Aswage] × 100

Important: These calculations compare theoretical cross-sectional areas at constant length. Actual forging may produce elongation, shortening, flash, scale loss, or incomplete die filling. The calculator does not simulate metal flow or predict the location of unfilled regions.
