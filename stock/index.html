FORGING CALCULATORS / STOCK DRAWING

Stock Drawing Calculator

Calculate how stock length changes when forging between square, rectangular, round, and octagonal cross-sections using conservation of volume.

How the Calculation Works

When forging steel from one cross-sectional size to another, the material changes shape, but its total volume remains approximately the same. As the cross-sectional area decreases, the length increases. This is the principle of conservation of volume.

1. Calculate the starting volume

For square or rectangular stock, multiply width, height (thickness), and length:

W × H × L = V

W = width; H = height (thickness); L = length; V = volume.

Example: A 1" × 1" square bar, 4" long:

1" × 1" × 4" = 4 in³

2. Conservation of volume

During forging, the steel is redistributed rather than intentionally removed. Ignoring material loss, the original and finished volumes are equal:

Vstarting = Vfinished

3. Calculate the finished length

Divide the original volume by the finished cross-sectional area (A):

Lfinished = Vstarting ÷ Afinished

Example: Forge a 1" square bar, 4" long, into a ¾" square bar.

Finished area: 0.75" × 0.75" = 0.5625 in²

Finished length: 4 in³ ÷ 0.5625 in² = 7.111"

The bar increases from 4" to approximately 7.111", a 77.8% increase in length.

Additional reference: stock shapes and material allowance

4. Cross-sectional area formulas

For shapes other than squares and rectangles, calculate the cross-sectional area first, then multiply it by length to find volume: V = A × L.

Stock shapeCross-sectional area (A)
SquareW²
RectangleW × H
Roundπ × D² ÷ 4
Regular octagon2 × (√2 − 1) × F²

D = diameter; F = distance across flats of a regular octagon.

5. Accounting for material loss

Scale, cleanup, and finishing may reduce the amount of usable steel. The calculator can add an extra percentage of starting stock:

Lprepare = Lrequired × (1 + P ÷ 100)

P = additional material allowance (%). For a theoretical starting length of 4" and a 5% allowance:

4" × 1.05 = 4.200" to prepare.

Important: These are theoretical calculations for uniform cross-sections. Actual forging may produce scale loss, uneven deformation, and dimensional variation.
